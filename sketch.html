Yibu as a paid product: sketch

A first outline to react to: voice conversations with the tutor at the centre, a free start limited only by an AI usage allowance, then a subscription for more time, with progress shown as the distance each learner has travelled.

1. The idea in one line

Voice conversations with the tutor are the heart of Yibu. Talk for free within a monthly allowance of AI usage; pay when you want more time with her.

Features are not locked behind the paywall. The tutor knows which words, phrases and sounds you own, which are slipping, and builds each session around that. Progress is the distance you have travelled in learning, not how fast you got there.

2. Tiers

Prices are placeholders to test, not researched figures.

Tier Price What it includes
Starter Free A monthly allowance of the tutor’s conversation and voice minutes (a placeholder amount to test). All languages and pairs, reviews, items and progress. When the allowance runs out, talking waits until it resets or the learner upgrades.
Plus €6.99 / month or €59 / year A higher monthly allowance of conversation and voice minutes. Same languages, items and features; more time with the tutor.
Later: Family or School Per seat Several learner profiles, a parent or teacher view of progress.

Reviews of already saved items stay available even after the talk allowance is used up, so the journey does not stall at the paywall. Prices are placeholders to test, not researched figures.

3. The free start

  1. Get to know you. The tutor asks conversationally about native, known and learning languages, and what the learner wants to practise (already live). No deadline and no date to hit.
  2. Talk, then gentle review. Voice conversation is the main thing. Due reviews are short and gentle, with no guilt if a session is missed, and talk then reuses those items — at the learner’s pace, not a daily time box.
  3. Optional reminder. An optional reminder at the learner’s chosen time, with no streak and no penalty for skipping.
  4. Distance travelled. Progress is the journey so far: words, phrases and meanings mastered, conversations held, hours spoken. Not a weekly deadline or a speed score.
  5. Upgrade moment. Offered when the monthly AI usage allowance is reached, never mid-sentence, with a 7-day Plus trial.

4. Languages

At launch, about 5 to 10 main languages. A proposed set is English, Hungarian, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean and Mandarin Chinese, with Portuguese as a possible tenth. Japanese, Korean and Mandarin are in from the start, not later add-ons.

That is not too many. Adding a language is mostly procedural: the pipeline is the same for every one (speech recognition, the model, and the tutor’s voice). Extra care is needed for Chinese, Japanese and Korean scripts, romanisation, tones and pitch, and for how well speech recognition handles each language, but those are notes on the same pipeline, not a different product.

Pairs are not prescribed. The learner picks any source language, or the target itself for an immersion-style session such as English from English, and any target, and may learn several at once: English from Hungarian, Chinese from English, or Chinese from Hungarian and English together. It is fine to start with just one. The tutor works this out conversationally at the start, as she already does.

5. Profiles and progress

Account. Email sign-in link (no password). Profiles belong to a person, not a machine: they are stored on the server and tied to the account. Today’s anonymous learner id and its memory move into the account on first sign-in.

Shared device. Two people can share one computer. A quick profile switcher lets each person open their own profile without mixing conversations or progress.

Cross-device. One person can use two computers, or a phone and a laptop. Signing in brings the same profiles, progress and conversation to each device.

Language profile. Any source and any target, or the target itself for immersion, and several at once if the learner wants that. Native, known and learning languages, editable by just telling the tutor. No prescribed pair and no deadline.

Learner model. Every tracked item with its review schedule, strength and history (see the next section).

Conversation. Each profile saves the conversation so far and remembers where it left off. Open the same profile on any device and the tutor picks up naturally, without starting over.

Distance travelled. A cumulative picture of the journey: words, phrases and meanings mastered, conversations held, and hours spoken. Not a streak, a deadline or a speed score.

History. Sessions, quiz scores and retention over time, as part of that same journey.

Your data. Export everything, and Forget me deletes it all (already live for the anonymous version).

6. Voice and conversation

Voice conversations are the core experience, not an add-on. Talking to the tutor should feel like a natural, casual back-and-forth with an assistant. The microphone listens continuously in the background; the learner never has to press, tap or hold a button to speak.

Listening. Voice activity detection notices when the learner starts speaking. End-of-turn detection notices when they have finished, so the tutor can reply without a button press.

Interrupting. The learner can cut in while the tutor is still speaking; she stops and listens to the new turn.

Echo cancellation. The tutor must not hear herself. Her own speech is filtered out so it is not treated as the learner talking.

Visible state. The interface always shows whether the tutor is listening, thinking or speaking. A mute or pause control is always at hand, so the learner can stop the mic without ending the session.

Silence. A short pause is just a pause: the tutor waits. After a longer silence she may check in briefly, or simply keep listening; the session is not ended and the conversation stays saved.

Hidden tab. If the tab is hidden or the app goes to the background, listening and speaking pause so the mic is not left on unattended. When the learner comes back, the session resumes from where it left off.

Typing fallback. Typing stays available at any time. Browsers without continuous speech recognition, including Firefox, fall back to the text box, and the rest of the conversation, profiles and resume behaviour stays the same.

7. What the learner knows

Item type Example Practised as
Word reliable Recognise, recall, say aloud
Phrase or chunk I’m looking forward to it Use it in conversation
Meaning unit bank (money) vs bank (river) Pick the right sense in context
Grammar pattern have been + -ing Build a sentence with it
Sound or stress PHOtograph vs phoTOgraphy Repeat and get feedback

Each item moves through new, learning, known and fluent; fluent means the learner produced it unprompted in speech. Each direction (recognise, produce, say aloud) has its own review schedule, the passive and active split from AngolMag.

Items come from the conversations themselves, as My words does today, tagged by language, never from a giant pre-made list.

8. How the tutor sequences practice

Talk (voice-first session) → Capture new items and errors → Schedule (FSRS per item) → Review (due items first) → Reuse (due items woven into talk) → Talk (next session; the loop continues).

  1. Due reviews come first, short and gentle, rated Again, Hard, Good or Easy (FSRS, aiming for 90% recall), with no guilt if some are left for later.
  2. The tutor then steers the conversation so that due and shaky items come up naturally, and counts a correct unprompted use as a strong review.
  3. New material is added only while recent retention stays high; if it drops, the session becomes more review and fewer new items.
  4. One grammar pattern at a time: introduced in a sentence, practised in talk, then scheduled like a word.
  5. Pronunciation: stress and sound errors that repeat across sessions become items of their own and get short repeat-after-me drills.
  6. Mixing: words, patterns and sounds are interleaved within a session rather than practised in blocks.

9. Build order

  1. Spaced repetition. FSRS scheduling on My words, a review screen with spoken prompts, taken at the learner’s pace (ideas taken from AngolMag, rebuilt for the site).
  2. Accounts. Email sign-in, per-person profiles stored on the server, move anonymous data into the account, conversation resume across devices, export and delete.
  3. Richer items. Phrases, meaning units and grammar patterns, and the tutor weaving due items into conversation.
  4. Languages. Start with one, such as English from English or from Hungarian, then add the rest of the launch set (about 5 to 10). Pairs are any-to-any, not prescribed. The pipeline is the same for each language, with extra notes for CJK scripts, romanisation, tones and pitch, and recognition quality.
  5. Paywall. A monthly AI usage allowance on the free tier, Plus raises that allowance via a payment provider, 7-day trial, paid Cloudflare plan to lift the free write limit.
  6. Pronunciation. Stress and sound feedback from the learner’s recordings.
