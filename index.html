Yibu

Yibu is a voice-first language-learning web app with an AI tutor and an English and Hungarian interface. The live site and the yibu repository currently include:

  • Talking with the tutor out loud or in writing. The microphone is always on; she answers aloud, and you can follow the words as they are spoken. Typing is still available.
  • A chat with honest, clear corrections. She is a bit strict, never harsh: mistakes that matter are fixed, small slips wait for a pause, and she learns how you like to be corrected.
  • A language picker for the language you are learning, and chat about your own interests rather than a fixed lesson plan.
  • My words — useful words and phrases saved from the chats.
  • A quiz built from those saved words.
  • A light and dark look. Dark mode follows the device, with a DRK / LGT control to override it.

Some related changes may still be deploying. The list above is what the live page showed when this text was checked.

The tutor backend

The Yibu page talks to a Cloudflare Worker named maria-tutor. At a high level the worker:

  • Handles the chat: it receives a turn from the page, builds the tutor’s reply, and returns it to the browser.
  • Serves quizzes drawn from the learner’s saved words.
  • Speaks replies with neural text-to-speech (the Iris voice).

Default name: tutor; a learner can give her a name. The worker also keeps a short memory of the conversation and saved words for each anonymous visitor, applies daily usage limits, and only answers the sites that are allowed to call it. It is an API the Yibu page uses, not a page to open on its own.

AngolMag

AngolMag is a Flutter app for learning English vocabulary with spaced repetition (FSRS). The source is the hive_again project. Its own design is Material, with an amber background and amber top bar, and a dark theme that follows the device.

  • A home screen with two cards: Passzív szókincs (passive vocabulary) and Aktív szókincs (active vocabulary), each with due counts.
  • A review screen with four ratings: újra, nehéz, jó and könnyű.

An Android APK of the app has been built. A web build is published at guywitharicecooker.github.io/AngolMag. The public repository is GuyWithARiceCooker/angolmag.

Yibu as a paid product

This is the product direction from the sketch, not a description of what the live app already charges for. Voice conversation with the tutor is at the centre.

  • The free tier is a monthly allowance of AI usage (conversation and voice time). Features are not locked behind the paywall; when the allowance runs out, talking waits until it resets or the learner upgrades. Reviews of already saved items stay available.
  • Progress is shown as the distance travelled — words, phrases and meanings mastered, conversations held, hours spoken — without time pressure, streaks or a speed score.
  • Launch is planned around 5 to 10 main languages, including Japanese, Korean and Mandarin Chinese from the start. A proposed set is English, Hungarian, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean and Mandarin, with Portuguese as a possible tenth.
  • Language pairs are any-to-any: the learner picks any source language (or the target itself, for an immersion-style session) and any target, and may learn several at once.
  • Profiles belong to a person, not a machine. Signing in brings the same profiles, progress and conversation to each device, so work can be resumed across a phone and a laptop, or switched between people who share one computer.

The full outline, including tiers, the free start, how practice is sequenced, and open questions, is in the paid-product sketch.
